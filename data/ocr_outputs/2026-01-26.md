## Backpropagation

*(Hand-drawn diagram of a neuron with inputs $y_0=1, y_1, y_2, \dots, y_n$ with weights $w_0, w_1, w_2, \dots, w_n$ going into a circle $f(y_0, y_1, y_2, \dots, y_n)$ producing output $\hat{z}_k$)*

Consider $k^{th}$ artificial neuron in output layer.
Suppose this is part of a regression task with sum of squares error loss.

$$L = \frac{1}{2} \sum_d \sum_\ell (z_\ell - \hat{z}_\ell)^2$$

*   $\sum_d$: summation over training instances
*   $\sum_\ell$: summation over components

We want $\frac{\partial L}{\partial w_j}$ for all $j$.

$$s_k = \sum_\ell w_\ell y_\ell$$
$$\hat{z}_k = a(s_k)$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w_j} = \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial s_k} \right) \left( \frac{\partial s_k}{\partial w_j} \right)$$
$$\frac{\partial s_k}{\partial w_j} = y_j$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial s_k} = \left( \frac{\partial L}{\partial \hat{z}_k} \right) \left( \frac{\partial \hat{z}_k}{\partial s_k} \right)$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial s_k} = - \sum_d (z_k - \hat{z}_k) a'(s_k)$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial w_j} = \left[ - \sum_d (z_k - \hat{z}_k) a'(s_k) \right] y_j$$

$\frac{\partial L}{\partial s_k}$ will be useful for computing partial derivatives in other layers of the network.
