### VECTORS
**Definition:**
Quantities with direction and magnitude

### BASIC VECTOR OPERATIONS
**Sum of vectors:**
$$\vec{a} + \vec{b} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ w \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x + u \\ y + v \\ z + w \end{bmatrix}$$

**Scalar multiplication:**
$$c\vec{a} = c \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} cx \\ cy \\ cz \end{bmatrix}$$

**Inner product:**
$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = \begin{bmatrix} x & y & z \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ w \end{bmatrix} = xu + yv + wz$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 3*

---

### VECTOR NORMS
Measure the size of a vector

$$\|\vec{a}\|_p = \left( \sum_i |a_i|^p \right)^{1/p}$$

**Most commonly:**
$$\|\vec{a}\|_1 = \sum_i |a_i|$$
$$\|\vec{a}\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_i |a_i|^2}$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 4*

### SPECIAL VECTORS
**Unit Vector:**
A vector $\vec{a}$ whose norm $\|\vec{a}\| = 1$.

**Orthogonal:**
Two vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ whose inner product $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$.

### MATRICES
**Definition:**
A 2D array of numbers

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$$

### BASIC MATRIX OPERATIONS
**Transposition:**
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \rightarrow A^T = \begin{bmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{bmatrix}$$

**Addition:**
$$A + B = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} e & f \\ g & h \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a+e & b+f \\ c+g & d+h \end{bmatrix}$$

**Scalar multiplication:**
$$eA = e \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ae & be \\ ce & de \end{bmatrix}$$

**Multiplication:**
$$AB = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} e & f \\ g & h \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ae+bg & af+bh \\ ce+dg & cf+dh \end{bmatrix}$$

### MATRIX NORMS
Frobenius norm is most common

$$\|A\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i,j} |A_{i,j}|^2}$$

### SPECIAL MATRICES
**Diagonal matrix:**
A square matrix $D$ for which $D_{i,j} = 0$ if $i \neq j$.

**Identity matrix:**
A diagonal matrix $I$ where all non-zero elements are 1.

**Symmetric matrix:**
A matrix $A$ for which $A = A^T$.

**Orthogonal matrix:**
A matrix $A$ for which $AA^T = I$.

**Scalar multiplication:**

### MATRIX-VECTOR MULTIPLICATION
*   Treat the vector like matrix with one column

$$A\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ax + by \\ cx + dy \end{bmatrix}$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 12*

### TRANSFORMATIONS
**Scaling:**
$$A\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ax \\ dy \end{bmatrix}$$

**Rotation:**
$$A\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x\cos\theta - y\sin\theta \\ x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$$

**Linear:**
$$A\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ax + by \\ cx + dy \end{bmatrix}$$

### LINEAR SYSTEMS
Assuming $A$ and $\vec{b}$ are known...
$$A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$$

Or more explicitly...
$$\begin{bmatrix} A_{1,1} & A_{1,2} \\ A_{2,1} & A_{2,2} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$A_{1,1}x_1 + A_{1,2}x_2 = b_1$$
$$A_{2,1}x_1 + A_{2,2}x_2 = b_2$$

[Note: OCR shows $b_1$ on the second line, but logically it is usually $b_2$]

### INVERSION FOR LINEAR SYSTEMS
For a matrix $A$, its inverse is called $A^{-1}$, and is a matrix satisfying
$$AA^{-1} = I$$

We can solve a linear system $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ as
$$\vec{x} = A^{-1}\vec{b}$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 15*

### LINEAR DEPENDENCE
**Linear combination:**
A linear combination of a set of vectors $\vec{a}_1, \vec{a}_2, \vec{a}_3, \dots$ is a sum of scalar multiples of these vectors $c_1\vec{a}_1 + c_2\vec{a}_2 + c_3\vec{a}_3 + \dots$

**Linear independent:**
A set of vectors $\vec{a}_1, \vec{a}_2, \vec{a}_3, \dots$ for which no vectors can be written as a linear combination of other vectors.

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 16*

---

### LINEAR DEPENDENCE IN LINEAR SYSTEMS
A linear system $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ has a unique solution if the number of linearly independent columns in $A$ is equal to the number of elements in $\vec{b}$.

### EIGENDECOMPOSITION
If $V$ is a matrix whose columns are eigenvectors of $A$
If $\Lambda$ is a diagonal matrix whose elements are eigenvalues of $A$

The eigendecomposition of a matrix $A$ is given by
$$A = V\Lambda V^{-1}$$

If $A$ is symmetric, then $V$ is orthogonal
$$A = V\Lambda V^T$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 20*

### TRACE AND DETERMINANT
*   Quantify how big a matrix

**Trace:**
Sum of diagonal elements (or sum of eigenvalues)
$$Tr(A) = \sum_i A_{i,i} = \sum_i \lambda_i$$

**Determinant:**
Product of eigenvalues
$$\det(A) = \prod_i \lambda_i$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 21*

### SINGULAR VALUE DECOMPOSITION
*   Similar to eigendecomposition, but works for non-square matrices

**Right singular vectors:**
Eigenvectors of $A^T A$

**Left singular vectors:**
Eigenvectors of $AA^T$

**Singular values:**
Square roots of eigenvalues of $A^T A$ or $AA^T$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 22*

---

### SINGULAR VALUE DECOMPOSITION
If $V$ is a matrix whose columns are right singular vectors of $A$
If $U$ is a matrix whose columns are left singular vectors of $A$
If $D$ is a diagonal matrix whose elements are singular values of $A$

The singular value decomposition of a matrix $A$ is given by
$$A = UDV^T$$

### MATRIX PSEUDOINVERSE
Assuming $A$ and $\vec{b}$ are known...
$$A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$$

Want to find some matrix $A^+$ such that
$$\vec{x} = A^+\vec{b}$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 24*

---

### MATRIX PSEUDOINVERSE
If $V$ is a matrix whose columns are right singular vectors of $A$
If $U$ is a matrix whose columns are left singular vectors of $A$
If $D^+$ is a diagonal matrix whose elements are the reciprocals of the singular values of $A$

The Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of a matrix $A$ is given by:
$$A^+ = VD^+U^T$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 25*

---

### MATRIX PSEUDOINVERSE
The solution to our system is:
$$\vec{x} = A^+\vec{b}$$

If the system is underdetermined, $\vec{x}$ is the solution with smallest L2 norm.
If the system is overdetermined, $\vec{x}$ gives us the closest possible solution.
