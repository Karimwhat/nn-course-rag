## Contractive Autoencoders
$\tilde{L} = L(x, g(f(x))) + \lambda \Omega(f(x), x)$  
$\uparrow$ derivative penalty

$\Omega(f(x), x)$ magnitude of derivatives of encoding w.r.t. inputs.  
$\Omega(h, x) = \sum_i \sum_j \left[ \left( \frac{\partial h_i}{\partial x_j} \right)^2 \right]$

## Convolutional Autoencoders
Encoder: $h : f(x)$ is a CNN  
Decoder: $\hat{x} : g(h)$ is a CNN

Encoder: Convolution + Pooling  
Decoder: Transposed convolution + Unpooling

### Transposed Convolution:
How to do opposite of convolution with stride?  
Insert zeros between all elements in all dimensions of the input. Then regular convolution.

Given a convolution with stride $s$.

## Unpooling
Upsampling an image to increase its size.  
Imagine we have a pooling operation with kernel size and stride of $s$.

### 1. Nearest neighbour:
$y(i, j) = x(\text{floor}(i/s), \text{floor}(j/s))$

$x: \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix} \quad y: \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 1 & 1 & 2 & 2 \\ 3 & 3 & 4 & 4 \\ 3 & 3 & 4 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$

### 2. Bed of nails
$y(i, j) = \begin{cases} x(i/s, j/s) & \text{if } i/s, j/s \in \mathbb{Z} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$

### 3. Max Unpooling
$y(i, j) = \begin{cases} x(k, \ell) & \text{if } i, j \text{ was at a maximum in corresponding pooling operation} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$

## Recurrent Autoencoder
Encoder: $h = f(x)$ is an many-to-one RNN  
Decoder: $\hat{x} = g(h)$ is an one-to-many RNN

The hidden state $h_T$ from the RNN encoder is our encoding.
