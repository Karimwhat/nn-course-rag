### PROBABILITY MASS FUNCTIONS (DISCRETE)
*   Indicates the probability a random variable takes on a value
$$P(X = x)$$

**Properties:**
$$\forall x, 0 \le P(x) \le 1$$
$$\sum_x P(x) = 1$$

### MARGINAL PROBABILITIES
*   A probability distribution computed over a subset of variables

**For discrete variables:**
$$\forall x, P(X = x) = \sum_y P(X = x, Y = y)$$

**For continuous variables:**
$$p(x) = \int p(x, y)dy$$

### CONDITIONAL PROBABILITY
*   Describes the probability one event occurs, given another event has occurred

$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(A, B)}{P(B)}$$

$$P(Y = y|X = x) = \frac{P(Y = y, X = x)}{P(X = x)}$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 7*

---

### INDEPENDENCE
*   Two events or random variables are independent if information about one does not provide information about the other

$$P(A|B) = P(A) \text{ or } P(B|A) = P(B)$$
$$P(A, B) = P(A)P(B)$$

$$\forall x, y \ P(X = x | Y = y) = P(X = x) \text{ or } \forall x, y \ P(Y = y | X = x) = P(Y = y)$$
$$\forall x, y \ P(X = x, Y = y) = P(X = x)P(Y = y)$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 8*

### CONDITIONAL INDEPENDENCE
*   Two events or random variables are conditionally independent given another if information about one does not provide information about the other

$$P(A, B | C) = P(A | C)P(B | C)$$

$$\forall x, y \ P(X = x, Y = y | Z = z) = P(X = x | Z = z)P(Y = y | Z = z)$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 9*

### CHAIN RULE OF CONDITIONAL PROBABILITY
*   From our definition of conditional probability we get

$$P(A, B) = P(A|B)P(B)$$
$$P(Y = y, X = x) = P(Y = y | X = x)P(X = x)$$

**In general, the chain rule of conditional probability is:**
$$P(A_1, \dots, A_n) = \prod_{i=1 \dots n} P(A_i | A_1, \dots, A_{i-1})$$

$$P(X_1 = x_1, \dots, X_n = x_n) = \prod_{i=1 \dots n} P(X_i = x_i | X_1 = x_1, \dots, X_{i-1} = x_{i-1})$$

### EXPECTED VALUE
*   The mean value a function of a random variable takes on when the random variable is drawn from a particular distribution

$$E_{X \sim P}[f(x)] = \sum_x P(X = x)f(x)$$

$$E_{x \sim p}[f(x)] = \int p(x)f(x)dx$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 11*

### VARIANCE
*   The spread a function of a random variable takes on when the random variable is drawn from a particular distribution

$$Var[f(x)] = E[(f(x) - E[f(x)])^2]$$

*January 7, 2026 | Matthew S. Holden, School of Computer Science, Carleton University | 12*

### COVARIANCE
*   Estimates the linear relationship between two functions of random variables

$$cov(f(x), g(y)) = E[(f(x) - E[f(x)])(g(y) - E[g(y)])]$$

### BAYES THEOREM
*   Tells us conditional probabilities in terms of other conditional probabilities

$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)}$$

Alternatively, if we think of an event $E$ as evidence and an event $H$ as a hypothesis we get
$$P(H|E) = \frac{P(E|H)P(H)}{P(E)}$$
