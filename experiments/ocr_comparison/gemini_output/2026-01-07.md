### VECTORS
**Definition:**
Quantities with direction and magnitude

[Diagram: A vector arrow $\vec{a}$ in a 2D coordinate system]

$$\vec{a} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}$$

---

### BASIC VECTOR OPERATIONS
**Sum of vectors:**
$$\vec{a} + \vec{b} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ w \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x + u \\ y + v \\ z + w \end{bmatrix}$$

**Scalar multiplication:**
$$c\vec{a} = c \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} cx \\ cy \\ cz \end{bmatrix}$$

**Inner product:**
$$\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = \begin{bmatrix} x & y & z \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ w \end{bmatrix} = xu + yv + wz$$

---

### VECTOR NORMS
Measure the size of a vector

$$\|\vec{a}\|_p = \left( \sum_i |a_i|^p \right)^{1/p}$$

**Most commonly:**
$$\|\vec{a}\|_1 = \sum_i |a_i|$$
$$\|\vec{a}\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_i |a_i|^2}$$

---

### SPECIAL VECTORS
**Unit Vector:**
A vector $\vec{a}$ whose norm $\|\vec{a}\| = 1$.

**Orthogonal:**
Two vectors $\vec{a}, \vec{b}$ whose inner product $\vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = 0$.

---

### MATRICES
**Definition:**
A 2D array of numbers

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$$

---

### BASIC MATRIX OPERATIONS
**Transposition:**
$$A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \rightarrow A^T = \begin{bmatrix} a & c \\ b & d \end{bmatrix}$$

**Addition:**
$$A + B = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} e & f \\ g & h \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} a+e & b+f \\ c+g & d+h \end{bmatrix}$$

**Scalar multiplication:**
$$eA = e \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ae & be \\ ce & de \end{bmatrix}$$

**Multiplication:**
$$AB = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} e & f \\ g & h \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ae+bg & af+bh \\ ce+dg & cf+dh \end{bmatrix}$$

---

### MATRIX NORMS
Frobenius norm is most common

$$\|A\|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{i,j} |A_{i,j}|^2}$$

---

### SPECIAL MATRICES
**Diagonal matrix:**
A square matrix $D$ for which $D_{i,j} = 0$ if $i \neq j$.

**Identity matrix:**
A diagonal matrix $I$ where all non-zero elements are 1.

**Symmetric matrix:**
A matrix $A$ for which $A = A^T$.

**Orthogonal matrix:**
A matrix $A$ for which $AA^T = I$.

---

### TENSORS
**Definition:**
An ND array of numbers (e.g. 3D)

$$A = \left[ \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} ; \begin{bmatrix} e & f \\ g & h \end{bmatrix} \right]$$

---

### BASIC TENSOR OPERATIONS
**Addition:**
$$A + B = \left[ \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} ; \begin{bmatrix} e & f \\ g & h \end{bmatrix} \right] + \left[ \begin{bmatrix} i & j \\ k & l \end{bmatrix} ; \begin{bmatrix} m & n \\ o & p \end{bmatrix} \right] = \left[ \begin{bmatrix} a+i & b+j \\ c+k & d+l \end{bmatrix} ; \begin{bmatrix} e+m & f+n \\ g+o & h+p \end{bmatrix} \right]$$

**Scalar multiplication:**
$$iA = i \left[ \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} ; \begin{bmatrix} e & f \\ g & h \end{bmatrix} \right] = \left[ \begin{bmatrix} ai & bi \\ ci & di \end{bmatrix} ; \begin{bmatrix} ei & fi \\ gi & hi \end{bmatrix} \right]$$

**Multiplication:**
There are several different ways, but get messy...

---

### MATRIX-VECTOR MULTIPLICATION
*   Treat the vector like matrix with one column

$$A\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ax + by \\ cx + dy \end{bmatrix}$$

---

### TRANSFORMATIONS
**Scaling:**
$$A\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} a & 0 \\ 0 & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ax \\ dy \end{bmatrix}$$

**Rotation:**
$$A\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} x\cos\theta - y\sin\theta \\ x\sin\theta + y\cos\theta \end{bmatrix}$$

**Linear:**
$$A\vec{v} = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} ax + by \\ cx + dy \end{bmatrix}$$

---

### LINEAR SYSTEMS
Assuming $A$ and $\vec{b}$ are known...
$$A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$$

Or more explicitly...
$$\begin{bmatrix} A_{1,1} & A_{1,2} \\ A_{2,1} & A_{2,2} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$A_{1,1}x_1 + A_{1,2}x_2 = b_1$$
$$A_{2,1}x_1 + A_{2,2}x_2 = b_2$$

[Note: OCR shows $b_1$ on the second line, but logically it is usually $b_2$]

---

### INVERSION FOR LINEAR SYSTEMS
For a matrix $A$, its inverse is called $A^{-1}$, and is a matrix satisfying
$$AA^{-1} = I$$

We can solve a linear system $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ as
$$\vec{x} = A^{-1}\vec{b}$$

---

### LINEAR DEPENDENCE
**Linear combination:**
A linear combination of a set of vectors $\vec{a}_1, \vec{a}_2, \vec{a}_3, \dots$ is a sum of scalar multiples of these vectors $c_1\vec{a}_1 + c_2\vec{a}_2 + c_3\vec{a}_3 + \dots$

**Linear independent:**
A set of vectors $\vec{a}_1, \vec{a}_2, \vec{a}_3, \dots$ for which no vectors can be written as a linear combination of other vectors.

---

### LINEAR DEPENDENCE IN LINEAR SYSTEMS
A linear system $A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$ has a unique solution if the number of linearly independent columns in $A$ is equal to the number of elements in $\vec{b}$.

---

### EIGENDECOMPOSITION
*   An efficient way of decomposing a square matrix into its parts
*   Useful to understand the properties of matrices

**Eigenvectors:**
Solutions $\vec{v}$ to the system $A\vec{v} = \lambda\vec{v}$.

**Eigenvalues:**
Coefficients $\lambda$ associated with eigenvalues

---

### ILLUSTRATION OF EIGENVALUES
[Diagram showing transformations of vectors $v^{(1)}$ and $v^{(2)}$ "Before multiplication" inside a unit circle and "After multiplication" where they are scaled into an ellipse by $\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$]

---

### EIGENDECOMPOSITION
If $V$ is a matrix whose columns are eigenvectors of $A$
If $\Lambda$ is a diagonal matrix whose elements are eigenvalues of $A$

The eigendecomposition of a matrix $A$ is given by
$$A = V\Lambda V^{-1}$$

If $A$ is symmetric, then $V$ is orthogonal
$$A = V\Lambda V^T$$

---

### TRACE AND DETERMINANT
*   Quantify how big a matrix

**Trace:**
Sum of diagonal elements (or sum of eigenvalues)
$$Tr(A) = \sum_i A_{i,i} = \sum_i \lambda_i$$

**Determinant:**
Product of eigenvalues
$$\det(A) = \prod_i \lambda_i$$

---

### SINGULAR VALUE DECOMPOSITION
*   Similar to eigendecomposition, but works for non-square matrices

**Right singular vectors:**
Eigenvectors of $A^T A$

**Left singular vectors:**
Eigenvectors of $AA^T$

**Singular values:**
Square roots of eigenvalues of $A^T A$ or $AA^T$

---

### SINGULAR VALUE DECOMPOSITION
If $V$ is a matrix whose columns are right singular vectors of $A$
If $U$ is a matrix whose columns are left singular vectors of $A$
If $D$ is a diagonal matrix whose elements are singular values of $A$

The singular value decomposition of a matrix $A$ is given by
$$A = UDV^T$$

---

### MATRIX PSEUDOINVERSE
Assuming $A$ and $\vec{b}$ are known...
$$A\vec{x} = \vec{b}$$

Want to find some matrix $A^+$ such that
$$\vec{x} = A^+\vec{b}$$

---

### MATRIX PSEUDOINVERSE
If $V$ is a matrix whose columns are right singular vectors of $A$
If $U$ is a matrix whose columns are left singular vectors of $A$
If $D^+$ is a diagonal matrix whose elements are the reciprocals of the singular values of $A$

The Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of a matrix $A$ is given by:
$$A^+ = VD^+U^T$$

---

### MATRIX PSEUDOINVERSE
The solution to our system is:
$$\vec{x} = A^+\vec{b}$$

If the system is underdetermined, $\vec{x}$ is the solution with smallest L2 norm.
If the system is overdetermined, $\vec{x}$ gives us the closest possible solution.

### BINARY CLASSIFICATION
$$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{\text{# correct predictions}}{\text{# predictions}}$$

$$\text{Sensitivity} = \text{Recall} = \frac{\text{# true positives}}{\text{# positives}}$$

$$\text{Specificity} = \frac{\text{# true negatives}}{\text{# negatives}}$$

$$\text{Precision} = \frac{\text{# true positives}}{\text{# predicted positives}}$$

$$F_1 = 2 \left( \frac{\text{precision} \times \text{recall}}{\text{precision} + \text{recall}} \right)$$

### “MACRO” AVERAGE
$$\text{MacroAccuracy} = \frac{1}{|\text{classes}|} \sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \frac{\text{# correct predictions for } c}{\text{# predictions for } c}$$

$$\text{MacroSensitivity} = \text{MacroRecall} = \frac{1}{|\text{classes}|} \sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \frac{\text{# true positives for } c}{\text{# positives for } c}$$

$$\text{MacroSpecificity} = \frac{1}{|\text{classes}|} \sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \frac{\text{# true negatives for } c}{\text{# negatives for } c}$$

$$\text{MacroPrecision} = \frac{1}{|\text{classes}|} \sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \frac{\text{# true positives for } c}{\text{# predicted positives for } c}$$

$$\text{Macro} F_1 = 2 \left( \frac{\text{macroprecision} \times \text{macrorecall}}{\text{macroprecision} + \text{macrorecall}} \right)$$

---

### “MICRO” AVERAGE
$$\text{MicroAccuracy} = \frac{\sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \text{# correct predictions for } c}{\sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \text{# predictions for } c}$$

$$\text{MicroSensitivity} = \text{MicroRecall} = \frac{\sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \text{# true positives for } c}{\sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \text{# positives for } c}$$

$$\text{MicroSpecificity} = \frac{\sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \text{# true negatives for } c}{\sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \text{# negatives for } c}$$

$$\text{MicroPrecision} = \frac{\sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \text{# true positives for } c}{\sum_{c \in \text{classes}} \text{# predicted positives for } c}$$

$$\text{Micro} F_1 = 2 \left( \frac{\text{microprecision} \times \text{microrecall}}{\text{microprecision} + \text{microrecall}} \right)$$

### HOLD-OUT VALIDATION
Assume we have $N$ samples, called $x_i$.
Split $x_i$ into 3 different sets: training, validation, test.

**for** $\theta$ in all combinations of hyperparameters:
train model $M(\theta)$ on training set
validate $M(\theta)$ on validation set

test $M(\theta)$ on test set using $\theta$ achieving best performance on validation set

---

### LEAVE-ONE-OUT CROSS-VALIDATION
Assume we have $N$ samples, called $x_i$.

**for** $i = 1 \dots N$:
train model $M$ on all samples $x_k$ for $k \neq i$
test $M$ on $x_i$

Performance is mean over all $x_i$

---

### K-FOLD CROSS-VALIDATION
Assume we have $N$ samples, called $x_i$.
Split $x_i$ into $k$ different folds $f_i$.

**for** $i = 1 \dots k$:
split remaining data into training set and validation set
**for** $\theta$ in all combinations of hyperparameters:
train model $M(\theta)$ on current training set
validate $M(\theta)$ on current validation set

test $M(\theta)$ on $f_i$ using $\theta$ achieving best performance on validation set

test performance is mean over all test folds

# PREREQUISITE REVIEW: PROBABILITY

### PROBABILITY
*   Provides a formal way to quantify uncertainty
*   Three sources:
    *   Stochasticity in the environment
    *   Partial observation of the environment
    *   Incomplete modelling of the environment

---

### PROBABILITY INTERPRETATIONS
**Frequentist probability:**
Analyzing the frequency of events

**Bayesian probability:**
Modelling degree of certainty/belief

---

### RANDOM VARIABLE
**Random Variable:**
A function that maps outcomes in a sample space to real numbers

**Example:**
*   Sum of values of two rolled dice
*   Distance between two particles in an accelerator

---

### PROBABILITY MASS FUNCTIONS (DISCRETE)
*   Indicates the probability a random variable takes on a value
$$P(X = x)$$

**Properties:**
$$\forall x, 0 \le P(x) \le 1$$
$$\sum_x P(x) = 1$$

---

### PROBABILITY DENSITY FUNCTIONS (CONT)
*   Is proportional to the probability of landing inside an infinitely small region at a specific value

**Properties:**
$$\forall x, p(x) \ge 0$$
$$\int p(x)dx = 1$$

---

### MARGINAL PROBABILITIES
*   A probability distribution computed over a subset of variables

**For discrete variables:**
$$\forall x, P(X = x) = \sum_y P(X = x, Y = y)$$

**For continuous variables:**
$$p(x) = \int p(x, y)dy$$

---

### CONDITIONAL PROBABILITY
*   Describes the probability one event occurs, given another event has occurred

$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(A, B)}{P(B)}$$

$$P(Y = y|X = x) = \frac{P(Y = y, X = x)}{P(X = x)}$$

---

### INDEPENDENCE
*   Two events or random variables are independent if information about one does not provide information about the other

$$P(A|B) = P(A) \text{ or } P(B|A) = P(B)$$
$$P(A, B) = P(A)P(B)$$

$$\forall x, y \ P(X = x | Y = y) = P(X = x) \text{ or } \forall x, y \ P(Y = y | X = x) = P(Y = y)$$
$$\forall x, y \ P(X = x, Y = y) = P(X = x)P(Y = y)$$

---

### CONDITIONAL INDEPENDENCE
*   Two events or random variables are conditionally independent given another if information about one does not provide information about the other

$$P(A, B | C) = P(A | C)P(B | C)$$

$$\forall x, y \ P(X = x, Y = y | Z = z) = P(X = x | Z = z)P(Y = y | Z = z)$$

---

### CHAIN RULE OF CONDITIONAL PROBABILITY
*   From our definition of conditional probability we get

$$P(A, B) = P(A|B)P(B)$$
$$P(Y = y, X = x) = P(Y = y | X = x)P(X = x)$$

**In general, the chain rule of conditional probability is:**
$$P(A_1, \dots, A_n) = \prod_{i=1 \dots n} P(A_i | A_1, \dots, A_{i-1})$$

$$P(X_1 = x_1, \dots, X_n = x_n) = \prod_{i=1 \dots n} P(X_i = x_i | X_1 = x_1, \dots, X_{i-1} = x_{i-1})$$

---

### EXPECTED VALUE
*   The mean value a function of a random variable takes on when the random variable is drawn from a particular distribution

$$E_{X \sim P}[f(x)] = \sum_x P(X = x)f(x)$$

$$E_{x \sim p}[f(x)] = \int p(x)f(x)dx$$

---

### VARIANCE
*   The spread a function of a random variable takes on when the random variable is drawn from a particular distribution

$$Var[f(x)] = E[(f(x) - E[f(x)])^2]$$

---

### COVARIANCE
*   Estimates the linear relationship between two functions of random variables

$$cov(f(x), g(y)) = E[(f(x) - E[f(x)])(g(y) - E[g(y)])]$$

---

### BAYES THEOREM
*   Tells us conditional probabilities in terms of other conditional probabilities

$$P(A|B) = \frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)}$$

Alternatively, if we think of an event $E$ as evidence and an event $H$ as a hypothesis we get
$$P(H|E) = \frac{P(E|H)P(H)}{P(E)}$$

### CALCULUS
*   Differential calculus tells us the slope of a function

[Diagrams showing slope behaviors at:]

*   **Maximum** (Parabola opening down, dot at top)
*   **Saddle point** (Inflection point on an S-curve)

### COMMON RULES FOR DIFFERENTIATION
**Power rule:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$$

**Scalar multiplication rule:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}(cu) = c \frac{du}{dx}$$

**Sum rule:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}(u + v) = \frac{du}{dx} + \frac{dv}{dx}$$

**Difference rule:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}(u - v) = \frac{du}{dx} - \frac{dv}{dx}$$

---

### COMMON RULES FOR DIFFERENTIATION
**Product rule:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}(uv) = v \frac{du}{dx} + u \frac{dv}{dx}$$

**Quotient rule:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{u}{v}\right) = \frac{v \frac{du}{dx} - u \frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$$

**Exponential rule:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}(e^x) = e^x$$

**Logarithm rule:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\ln x) = \frac{1}{x}$$

---

### COMMON RULES FOR DIFFERENTIATION
**Trigonometry rules:**
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x$$
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\cos x) = -\sin x$$
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\tan x) = \sec^2 x$$
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\cot x) = -\csc^2 x$$
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\sec x) = \sec x \tan x$$
$$\frac{d}{dx}(\csc x) = -\csc x \cot x$$

---

### PARTIAL DERIVATIVES
*   A measurement of how a multi-variable function changes with respect to one variable

$$\frac{\partial f(x, y)}{\partial x} \text{ differentiates with } y \text{ held constant}$$

$$\frac{\partial f(x_1, \dots, x_n)}{\partial x_i} \text{ differentiates with all variables except } x_i \text{ held constant}$$

### GRADIENT
*   A vector of partial derivatives

$$\nabla f(x, y) = \left[ \frac{\partial f(x, y)}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f(x, y)}{\partial y} \right]$$

$$\nabla f(x_1, \dots, x_n) = \left[ \frac{\partial f(x_1, \dots, x_n)}{\partial x_1}, \dots, \frac{\partial f(x_1, \dots, x_n)}{\partial x_n} \right]$$
